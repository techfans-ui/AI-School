VA BENEFITS POLICY AND PROCEDURES GUIDE (SAMPLE)
For demonstration purposes only — not an official Department of Veterans Affairs document.

SECTION 1 — DISABILITY COMPENSATION

Disability compensation is a tax-free monetary benefit paid to veterans with
disabilities that are the result of a disease or injury incurred or aggravated
during active military service. To be eligible, a veteran must have a current
diagnosed condition, evidence of an in-service event or injury, and a medical
nexus linking the two. The amount of compensation is based on the combined
disability rating, expressed as a percentage from 10 percent to 100 percent in
increments of 10.

How to apply: Veterans may file a claim online through VA.gov, by mail using VA
Form 21-526EZ, or in person at a VA regional office. Supporting evidence such as
service treatment records, private medical records, and lay statements should be
submitted with the claim to reduce processing time. The Intent to File process
allows a veteran to establish an effective date while gathering evidence.

SECTION 2 — HEALTH CARE ENROLLMENT

Most veterans who served in the active military and were discharged under
conditions other than dishonorable may qualify for VA health care. Enrollment
priority is assigned into eight Priority Groups, with Priority Group 1 reserved
for veterans with service-connected disabilities rated 50 percent or more.
Veterans may apply for health care using VA Form 10-10EZ online, by phone, or at
any VA medical facility.

Copayments: Some veterans may be charged copayments for non-service-connected
care. Veterans rated 50 percent or higher, former prisoners of war, and those
with conditions related to military sexual trauma are generally exempt from
copayments for related care.

SECTION 3 — EDUCATION BENEFITS

The Post-9/11 GI Bill provides education benefits for veterans who served at
least 90 days of aggregate active duty after September 10, 2001. Benefits may
cover tuition and fees, a monthly housing allowance, and a stipend for books and
supplies. The percentage of benefits is tied to the total length of qualifying
active-duty service.

VET TEC: The Veteran Employment Through Technology Education Courses program
offers eligible veterans the opportunity to enroll in high-technology training
programs, including computer software, information science, data processing, and
media application. VET TEC does not count against GI Bill entitlement.

SECTION 4 — HOME LOAN GUARANTY

The VA home loan guaranty program helps eligible veterans, service members, and
surviving spouses obtain home loans with favorable terms, often with no down
payment and no private mortgage insurance. Eligibility requires a valid
Certificate of Eligibility (COE), which can be requested online, through a
lender, or by mail. A VA funding fee may apply, but veterans receiving
compensation for a service-connected disability are typically exempt from the fee.

SECTION 5 — APPEALS AND DECISION REVIEW

Veterans who disagree with a VA decision have three review options under the
Appeals Modernization Act: a Higher-Level Review, a Supplemental Claim, or an
appeal to the Board of Veterans' Appeals. A Higher-Level Review asks a senior
reviewer to re-examine the same evidence. A Supplemental Claim allows the veteran
to submit new and relevant evidence. The deadline to request a review is
generally one year from the date of the decision notice.

SECTION 6 — SURVIVOR BENEFITS

Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC) is a tax-free monetary benefit paid
to eligible survivors of service members who died in the line of duty or
veterans whose death resulted from a service-connected condition. Survivors may
apply using VA Form 21P-534EZ. Additional benefits may include a Survivors'
Pension and educational assistance under the Survivors' and Dependents'
Educational Assistance program.
